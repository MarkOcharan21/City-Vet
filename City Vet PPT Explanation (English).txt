============================
SLIDE 2 — RATIONALE
============================
(Explanation)

This is the "why we did this" slide — the problem and the research gap that our system
addresses. This is where you establish that the clinic faces a REAL, DEMONSTRABLE problem,
not an invented one.

What to say:
- The City Veterinary Animal Clinic of Cabuyao serves a large number of pet owners, yet its
  records are still largely manual — paper-based and handwritten.
- Because records are manual: processes are slow, data is prone to delays and inconsistencies,
  and it is difficult to access or retrieve information.
- A particularly painful problem is matching Treasury-issued Official Receipts against actual
  payments made at the clinic — the OR and the payment record exist in separate worlds.
- There is no proper vaccination tracking (who has been vaccinated, when the next dose is due)
  and no analytics that the clinic can use for planning.

The research gap:
- Existing systems are fragmented — one handles registration but not payments, another handles
  payments but not vaccinations, and so on. What is missing is a single, meaningful thing: one
  QR code that links together every record of one pet's registration, vaccination, payment,
  and clinical history.
- What is needed: one integrated web-based system built specifically for the City Veterinary
  Animal Clinic in Cabuyao.

For the panel (likely questions + answers):
- "What is the core problem you are solving?" → Fragmented records. One system; the QR binds
  the pet's entire history together.
- "Why web-based instead of desktop?" → Accessibility anywhere, and real-time access shared by
  Admin, Staff, Veterinarian, and Pet Owner.

Delivery tip: Do not reveal the solution here. Stand firmly on the severity of the problem;
the next slides will present what we did about it.

SLIDE 3 — OBJECTIVES
============================
(Explanation)

This is the "what we set out to do." Three levels: a General Objective, and then three specific
objectives — each one linked to a measurable result.

What to say:
- GENERAL: To implement and document one integrated web-based system for the City Veterinary
  Animal Clinic of Cabuyao — this is the full title of the study.
- OBJECTIVE 1 — Develop and implement the actual system:
  - role-based access for four groups (Admin, Staff, Veterinarian, Pet Owner),
  - owner and pet registration, a unique QR code per pet, and a digital pet booklet,
  - vaccination monitoring and a multi-step consultation workflow with medicine prescription,
  - Treasury-issued Official Receipt monitoring and offline draft registration (records can be
    captured even without internet).
- OBJECTIVE 2 — Centralized online system: one unified database linking owner, pet, payment,
  vaccination, clinical, and medicine records — plus role-specific dashboards, QR scanning,
  search and filter, analytics, and activity auditing.
- OBJECTIVE 3 — Evaluation: measure system quality using the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality
  Model. End users rate Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, and Usability; IT
  experts rate Reliability and Security.

For the panel:
- "Why ISO/IEC 25010?" → It is the recognized international standard for measuring software
  quality. More credible than a subjective "it seems fine."
- "How do you know you met each objective?" → Each one is measurable: Objective 1 via testing
  (17/17 unit, 8/8 integration) and Objective 3 via the questionnaires.

Delivery tip: Map the three objectives to the later slides — Slide 8 = Obj 1, Slide 9 = Obj 2,
Slides 10–11 = Obj 3. Tell the panel the evidence for each one is coming up.

============================
SLIDE 4 — SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS
============================
(Explanation)

This is the "this far, and no further" slide — it shows the panel that we know the boundaries
of the study and are not claiming to do everything.

Scope (what is included):
- Limited to the City Veterinary Animal Clinic of Cabuyao only — a single institution.
- Included: role-based access (Admin, Staff, Veterinarian, Pet Owner), owner and pet
  registration with QR generation and a digital pet booklet, vaccination monitoring, clinical
  and medicine records, payment monitoring via Official Receipts, analytics and reporting,
  notifications, outreach, activity audit, and offline drafts.

Limitations (what is NOT included, and why):
- Results cannot be generalized to other clinics or institutions.
- Web-based only; no native Android or iOS application.
- No pharmacy/inventory management, lab testing, confinement, or surgery management — these are
  not part of the clinic's current process.
- No full appointment scheduling; offline drafts are saved only on the device (no live sync
  while offline).
- No direct Treasury integration; Official Receipts are recorded manually in the system.

For the panel:
- "Why only one clinic?" → To test the system deeply and properly. Generalization is left to
  future studies (also addressed in the Slide 12 recommendations).

Delivery tip: Do not defend the limitations as if they were shortcomings. Frame them as
deliberate scope decisions and point out they are addressed in the recommendations — this
shows responsible scoping.

SLIDE 5 — METHODOLOGY
============================
(Explanation)

This is the "how we did the study." It combines two designs — Developmental Research (the
deliverable IS a product) and Agile Scrum (for the development process) — using a mixed-method
approach (qualitative + quantitative).

What to say:
- We used a developmental research design — the software itself is the output of the research,
  built using Agile Scrum so that every sprint produces a working increment.
- TWO PHASES:
  1. Pre-development (QUALITATIVE): interviews, consultations, and observation at the clinic —
     to learn the real workflow and problems before writing any code.
  2. Development: Agile Scrum in iterative sprints, each sprint delivering a working increment.
  3. Post-development (QUANTITATIVE): ISO/IEC 25010 evaluation using questionnaires on a
     five-point Likert scale.
- QA during development: 17 of 17 unit tests passed; 8 of 8 integration tests passed.
- RESPONDENTS: 55 purposely selected — 50 end users (staff + pet owners) and 5 IT experts.
  (This is the chart on the right.)

For the panel:
- "Why Agile Scrum and not Waterfall?" → Requirements evolve while building; Scrum gives early
  feedback and allows adjustments every sprint.
- "How did you choose respondents?" → Purposive sampling: 50 actual users of the system (staff
  and pet owners) and 5 IT experts with sufficient technical background, aligned with ISO/IEC
  25010 requirements.
- "What are the 55?" → 50 end users rating Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, and
  Usability; 5 IT experts rating Reliability and Security.

SLIDE 6 — EVIDENCE OF METHODOLOGY (Agile Scrum execution)
============================
(Explanation)

This slide is PROOF that you actually followed Scrum — not just a claim on paper. It shows the
seven sprints and the Scrum events.

What to say (flow through the sprints):
- SPRINT 0 — Requirements: interviews, consultations, observation, and document analysis;
  creation of user stories and the product backlog.
- SPRINT 1 — Login and foundation: account management, role-based login, and stack setup
  (React frontend, Node.js backend, MySQL migrated to Supabase/PostgreSQL).
- SPRINT 2 — Registration and QR: OWNER and PET registration; a unique QR code per pet and the
  digital pet booklet.
- SPRINT 3 — Vaccination and payments: recording and monitoring vaccination history; payment
  monitoring with Treasury Official Receipts.
- SPRINT 4 — Clinical and medicine: multi-step consultation workflow and medicine prescription
  with auto-computed dosage.
- SPRINT 5 — Analytics and admin: analytics dashboards and reporting; activity audit, product
  catalog, and announcements.
- SPRINT 6 — Testing and UAT: unit and integration testing, followed by the ISO/IEC 25010
  evaluation by end users and IT experts.

SCRUM EVENTS (practiced every sprint):
- Sprint planning (define the sprint scope/backlog)
- Daily scrum (short daily stand-up meeting)
- Sprint review with stakeholders (demo the increment)
- Sprint retrospective (review and improve the process)

For the panel:
- "What evidence shows you were truly agile?" → A working increment per sprint plus the four
  Scrum events plus requirements adjustment (e.g., the offline draft feature was added after
  learning the clinic's real internet situation).
- Keep this slide crisp — naming each sprint and its deliverable is enough.

============================
SLIDE 10 — OBJECTIVE 3: END USER EVALUATION (ISO/IEC 25010)
============================
(Explanation)

Now the proof that the system is good according to its REAL USERS: 50 end users (clinic staff
and pet owners), five-point Likert scale.

What to say:
- RESULTS (weighted mean):
  - GRAND weighted mean = 4.25 → "Strongly Agree" (highly acceptable to end users).
  - HIGHEST characteristic: Performance Efficiency = 4.28 (Strongly Agree) — the system is fast
    and efficient in actual use.
  - 12 of the 18 indicators scored Strongly Agree.
- DISCUSSION: "The lowest items were vaccination history recording (4.04) and pet information
  presentation (4.10). But these are NOT core system defects — they signal a need for training
  and orientation so staff and veterinarians can use the vaccination module properly." (This
  becomes a recommendation on Slide 12.)
- CONCLUSION: "End users strongly agreed that the system is acceptable and that it serves the
  clinic's registration, identification, vaccination, and payment processes."

For the panel:
- "Only 4.25? Should it not be 5?" → 4.25 on a five-point Likert scale falls within the
  "Strongly Agree" band. In evaluation research, 4.20 and above is already a strong result —
  and our lower items have a REASON and an ACTION (training), not just an excuse.
- "What is the five-point scale?" → 1 = Strongly Disagree ... 5 = Strongly Agree; the weighted
  mean is the average score across all respondents per indicator.

SLIDE 11 — OBJECTIVE 3: IT EXPERT EVALUATION (ISO/IEC 25010:2023)
============================
(Explanation)

Double evidence: two groups evaluated the system — end users (Slide 10: 4.25) and now the
technical side (5 IT experts: 4.95). The expert score is higher because they rate the technical
quality.

What to say:
- RESULTS (n = 5, weighted mean):
  - OVERALL weighted mean = 4.95 → Strongly Agree (very high from technical evaluators).
  - HIGHEST: Usability = 5.00 (a perfect score for ease of use).
  - 22 of the 30 indicators scored a perfect 5.00.
- DISCUSSION: "Experts rated every quality characteristic as Strongly Agree. The LOWEST was
  Performance Efficiency at 4.87 — still Strongly Agree."
- CONCLUSION: "The system is highly acceptable to technical evaluators (4.95) and to end users
  (4.25). With both groups, the general objective of the study was met."

For the panel:
- "Why 4.95 from experts but 4.25 from end users?" → Normal: end users judge day-to-day
  workflow (so they notice smaller items like vaccination recording), while experts judge
  technical quality (reliability, security). The samples also differ (50 vs 5).
- "How did you apply ISO/IEC 25010:2023?" → Questionnaires built around the standard's quality
  characteristics — end users: Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability;
  experts: Reliability and Security (with some overlap).

============================
SLIDE 12 — RECOMMENDATIONS
============================
(Explanation)

This is the "what comes next based on our findings." Each FINDING (from the evaluation) has a
matching RECOMMENDATION — nothing is recommended without a basis.

What to say (five pairs):
1. FINDING: Vaccination history recording was the lowest-rated end-user item (4.04).
   → RECOMMENDATION: Strengthen the vaccination module; provide TRAINING and orientation for
     staff and veterinarians.
2. FINDING: Web-only access; offline drafts are device-local.
   → RECOMMENDATION: Develop a native mobile application and real-time synchronization across
     devices.
3. FINDING: Payments rely on manual Official Receipt recording.
   → RECOMMENDATION: Integrate an online payment gateway; notify owners of due vaccines and
     follow-ups via email or SMS.
4. FINDING: No medicine inventory, stock management, or custom reports yet.
   → RECOMMENDATION: Add medicine inventory and custom report generation with multiple export
     formats.
5. FINDING: The study covered one clinic and one research period.
   → RECOMMENDATION: Future studies — other clinics, long-term effects, and maintainability and
     portability assessment.

Closing line: "Keep the system as daily support to the clinic — with regular backups, audits,
and a contingency plan."

For the panel:
- "Where did these recommendations come from?" → Directly from the RESULTS — every finding has
  supporting evidence (e.g., the 4.04 vaccination score or the limitations in Slide 4). They
  are not arbitrary suggestions.
- "Which is your priority?" → Link it to the lowest score (vaccination training) and to the
  biggest daily-operations impact (payment gateway).
